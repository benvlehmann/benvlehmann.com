Glossary

The glossary records the renderings adopted for Spinoza’s technical vocabulary, with notes on the choices.

abiectio
self-abasement
Thinking less highly of oneself than is just, out of sadness (E3DA29), the opposite of pride; ‘despondency’ suggested hopelessness and sat close to desperatio (despair). Abiectus → ‘self-abased’.
absolute
absolutely
‘Without qualification’; in absolute infinitum opposed to in suo genere (infinite in its own kind).
acquiescentia in se ipso / animi
self-contentment / contentment of mind
Root sense is coming to rest (quies) in oneself. ‘Self-esteem’ imports a modern psychological sense.
actuosus
active
adaequatus
adequate
admiratio / contemptus
wonder / contempt
Wonder is not an affect (E3DA4Ex); note at E3P52S.
aemulatio / benevolentia
emulation / benevolence
affectio
affection
A state or modification of a thing; must be kept distinct from affectus.
affectus
affect
‘Affect’ keeps the link with affectio and covers active affects (E3P58–59), which ‘emotion’ and ‘passion’ do not.
affectuum imitatio
imitation of the affects
agere / actio
act / action
From E3D2, acting is being the adequate cause of an effect; opposed to pati.
ambitio / humanitas
ambition / courtesy
Humanitas is identified with modestia in E3DA43.
amor / odium
love / hatred
anima
soul
Rare; kept distinct from mens.
animi in periculis praesentia
presence of mind in dangers
animi pathema
passion of the mind
From the Greek πάθημα (‘something undergone’).
animositas / generositas
tenacity / nobility
appetitus
appetite
attributum
attribute
beatitudo
blessedness
beneplacitum
good pleasure
bonum / malum
good / evil
Defined relative to joy and desire in E3P39S, from the standpoint of reason in E4D1–2. ‘Bad’ where the context is non-moral.
causa efficiens
efficient cause
The producing cause, as distinct from material, formal and final causes.
causa finalis / finis
final cause / end
Propter finem → ‘for the sake of an end’.
causa immanens / transiens
immanent / transitive cause
causa per se / per accidens
cause through itself / by accident
causa sui
cause of itself
certus
certain
In the older sense ‘definite, fixed’ (certa ac determinata ratione, ‘in a certain and determinate manner’); ‘definite’ where ‘certain’ would mislead.
civitas / cives
state / citizens
classis / natio
class / nation
cogitatio / res cogitans
thought / thinking thing
cognitio
knowledge
Also ‘cognition’ is possible; ‘knowledge’ is used throughout, including for the three kinds of knowledge (E2P40S2).
commiseratio / misericordia
pity / compassion
E3DA18Ex: they differ only as the single affect and the habit; E3DA24 redefines misericordia, ‘against the meaning of the word’, as love that rejoices in another’s good and is saddened by his ill.
conatus / conari
striving / strive
The noun is also used untranslated in the notes and essays, as is customary.
conceptus / perceptio
concept / perception
concipere / conceptus
conceive / concept
constituere
constitute
contingens
contingent
contrarii affectus
contrary affects
convenire
agree
Also ‘agree in nature’ (E4P31–35).
corpus
body
crudelitas
cruelty
crudelitas seu saevitia
cruelty, or savagery
cupiditas
desire
dari / datur
be / there is (idiomatic)
Literally ‘to be given’. Rendered idiomatically (‘there is’, ‘there can be’, ‘exist’); ‘be given’ where Spinoza contrasts a given cause with its effect (ex data causa, E1A3).
decretum (mentis) / determinatio (corporis)
decree / determination
One thing under two attributes (E3P2S).
denominatio intrinseca / extrinseca
intrinsic / extrinsic denomination
desiderium
longing
determinare
determine
Deus
God
Deus seu natura
God, or nature
Only at E4Pref.2 (twice: Deum seu naturam, Deus seu natura) and E4P4D (Dei sive naturae, Dei seu naturae). The Dutch omits seu natura at E4Pref.2.
dictamen rationis
dictate of reason
Rationis dictamina (E4P18S): the precepts of reason stated in advance of Props. 19–37.
duratio
duration
ens
being
Also used for esse as a noun (realitas aut esse, ‘reality or being’, E1P9).
ens imaginationis
being of imagination
Coined against ens rationis; note at E1App.4.
ens rationis
being of reason
esse formale / esse obiectivum
formal being / objective being
Note at E2P5; see formaliter / obiective.
essentia
essence
exemplar
model
The universal idea against which things are judged perfect or imperfect; naturae humanae exemplar (E4Pref.3).
explicare
explain
Root sense ‘unfold’; note where the image matters.
exprimere
express
extensio / res extensa
extension / extended thing
fastidium / taedium
disgust / weariness
favor / indignatio
favour / indignation
felicitas
happiness
E4P18S: happiness consists in a man’s being able to preserve his being. Distinct from beatitudo (‘blessedness’).
fingere / fictio
feign / fiction
Technical sense from the Treatise on the Emendation of the Intellect: forming an idea that is not grounded in knowledge of the thing.
fluctuatio (imaginationis, animi)
fluctuation (of the imagination, of mind)
E3P17S: animi fluctuatio is related to the affect as doubt is to the imagination.
formaliter / obiective
formally / objectively
Scholastic-Cartesian pair: a thing as it is in itself vs. as it is represented in an idea. ‘Objectively’ is the reverse of the modern sense.
fortitudo
strength of character
gaudium / conscientiae morsus
gladness / remorse
Gaudium must be kept apart from laetitia (joy). Conscientiae morsus is not moral remorse but sadness at a past outcome contrary to hope (E3DA17); note at E3P18S2.
gloria / pudor
glory / shame
gratia / gratitudo
thankfulness / gratitude
homo
man / men
Generic, as the Note on the Text says; Spinoza’s word for a male is vir. ‘Man’ keeps the uniformity of phrases like homo liber, ‘the free man’, and hominem homini Deum esse, ‘man is a God to man’ (E4P35S).
honestas / honestum / turpe
honourableness / honourable / base
humilitas / philautia
humility / self-love
idea / ideatum
idea / object (of the idea)
Ideatum is ‘that of which the idea is an idea’; ‘object’ with a note at first occurrence.
imaginatio / imaginari
imagination / imagine
Technical: thinking through images (bodily affections), the first kind of knowledge (E2P17S, E2P40S2).
imperium
dominion / rule
‘Dominion’ in imperium in imperio, ‘a dominion within a dominion’ (E3Pref), and for the mind’s dominion over the affects or the body (E3P2S, E5Pref); ‘rule’ elsewhere (ex rationis imperio, ‘by the rule of reason’, E5P10S; E4App9, E4App13).
impietas
impiety
Opposed to pietas (‘morality’) at E4P18S.2; see the note there.
impossibilis
impossible
impotentia
lack of power
Opposed to potentia; Part IV calls human lack of power in moderating the affects bondage (E4Pref).
impudentia / clementia
shamelessness / clemency
in alio esse
be in another
in se esse
be in itself
in suo genere
in its own kind
infinitus
infinite
infinita attributa: ‘infinite attributes’, keeping the Latin’s ambiguity between ‘infinitely many’ and ‘each infinite’ (note at E1D6).
ingenium
temperament
A person’s characteristic make-up of mind; ‘ingenuity’ or ‘talent’ where it means wit or ability (E1App.2, E3Pref, E4App9).
intellectio
intellection
intellectus / intelligere
intellect / understand
intellectus actu / potentia
actual / potential intellect
Note at E1P31S.
intrepidus / audax / timidus / pusillanimis
fearless / daring / timid / cowardly
invidia
envy
involvere
involve
Conceptual containment: to conceive A one must conceive B. Never rendered ‘imply’; the one ‘implies’ in the translation renders implicat (Part I, Prop. 13, Schol.), the only occurrence of implicare.
ira / vindicta
anger / vengeance
irrisio / dedignatio
mockery / disdain
ius naturae
right of nature; natural right
Summo naturae iure → ‘by the highest right of nature’. Right is defined by power (E4P37S1; TP II.3).
iustum / iniustum
just / unjust
laetitia / tristitia
joy / sadness
Defined as the passage to greater or lesser perfection (E3P11S); ‘pleasure/pain’ suggests bodily sensation, which Spinoza names separately (titillatio, dolor).
laus / vituperium
praise / blame
liber / libertas
free / freedom
luxuria / ebrietas / libido / avaritia
gluttony / drunkenness / lust / greed
mens
mind
modificatio
modification
modus
mode
Also ‘way, manner’ in non-technical uses (nullo alio modo), translated accordingly.
natura
nature
in rerum natura: ‘in the nature of things’ (idiomatic for ‘in reality’); ‘nature’ is kept in lower case throughout, also in Deus seu natura (‘God, or nature’).
natura naturans / naturata
Natura naturans / Natura naturata
Left in Latin, as is customary, with a note.
necessitas / necessarius
necessity / necessary
norma
standard
veritas norma sui et falsi; compare Letter 76, verum index sui et falsi.
notiones communes
common notions
nutus (Dei)
bidding
ex solo Dei nutu agere; ‘will’ and ‘decree’ are kept for voluntas and decretum.
omen (bonum / malum)
(good / bad) omen
operari
operate
Kept distinct from agere (act); note at E1D7. ‘Operate’ when it stands alone, ‘produce an effect’ when it takes an object (note at E1P26).
pati / passio
be acted on / passion
peccatum / meritum
sin / merit
Disobedience / obedience in the civil state; extrinsic notions (E4P37S2.2).
per accidens
accidentally
per se concipi
be conceived through itself
per se notum / manifestum
self-evident
perfectio
perfection
pietas
piety
In Part IV, the desire to do good born of living by the guidance of reason (E4P37S1). The traditional word is kept, as with religio, and a note there gives Spinoza’s sense: not religious observance.
poenitentia
repentance
ponere / tollere
posit / take away
possibilis
possible
Distinguished from contingens in E4D3–4.
potentia
power
potestas
power
Spinoza distinguishes potentia (the power by which things are and act) from potestas (what is in someone’s power, at his disposal). Both are ‘power’, with notes where the distinction matters: E1P17S, E1P33S2.2, E1P35; E5Pref, E5P10.
praeiudicium
prejudice
propensio / aversio
inclination / aversion
quantum in se est
as far as it is in itself
The phrase of Descartes’ first law of nature (Principles II.37); note at E3P6.
quatenus
insofar as
ratio
reason
Also ‘manner, way’ (certa ac determinata ratione) and ‘account, ground’; causa seu ratio: ‘cause, or reason’.
realitas
reality
realiter distinctus
really distinct
The Cartesian ‘real distinction’ (between substances), opposed to modal distinction (E1P15S).
religio
religion
Whatever we desire and do of which we are the cause insofar as we know God (E4P37S1).
res
thing
res singularis
singular thing
scientia intuitiva
intuitive knowledge
securitas / desperatio
confidence / despair
sentire
feel / sense / think
By context: ‘feel’ for feeling and experience (E2A4–5, E5P23S), ‘sense’ for sense perception (E2P49S.3, E5Pref), ‘think’ for holding an opinion, above all de se plus (minus) iusto sentire, ‘think more (less) highly of oneself than is just’ (E3DA21–29).
servitus
bondage
Man’s lack of power in moderating and restraining the affects (E4Pref). Title of Part IV: De servitute humana. In the non-technical sense of E2P49S.4 (summa servitute; Dei servitus) rendered ‘servitude’.
spes / metus
hope / fear
status naturalis / civilis
natural / civil state
sub specie aeternitatis
under a species of eternity
First as sub quadam aeternitatis specie (‘under a certain species of eternity’); Part V drops quadam. Species is ‘look, aspect’ as well as ‘kind’.
substantia
substance
sui iuris
under his own control
Literally ‘of his own right’; see TP II.9–11 (note at E4Pref). Alterius iuris: ‘under another’s control’.
superbia / existimatio / despectus
pride / overestimation / scorn
All three are joys (E3P26S); despectus is kept apart from contemptus (contempt, E3DA5).
superstitio
superstition
suspendere iudicium / assentiri
suspend judgment / assent
temperantia / sobrietas / castitas
temperance / sobriety / chastity
terminare
limit
timor / verecundia / consternatio
timidity / shamefacedness / consternation
Timor is ‘timidity’ as the affect defined in E3DA39, and ‘fear’ where Spinoza uses it in its ordinary sense (E4P1S, with a note; E4P37S2.2, E4P63D, E4P63CS); ‘fear’ otherwise renders metus. Verecundia is kept apart from modestia.
titillatio / hilaritas / dolor / melancholia
pleasure / cheerfulness / pain / melancholy
Joy and sadness as related to one part of the body (titillatio, dolor) or to all parts equally (hilaritas, melancholia).
utile
advantage / useful
The noun (suum utile, commune utile) → ‘his own advantage’, ‘the common advantage’; the adjective → ‘useful’ (suum utile, quod revera utile est → ‘his own advantage, what is really useful to him’, E4P18S).
veneratio / horror / devotio
veneration / horror / devotion
virtus
virtue
Identified with power at E4D8. In Parts I–II, where virtus means causal efficacy (E1P11S), it is rendered ‘power’ with a note.
vitium
vice
voluntas / volitio
will / volition
zelotypia
jealousy
