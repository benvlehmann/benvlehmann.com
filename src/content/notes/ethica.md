I recently purchased a copy of Spinoza's *Ethics*. But as I started reading, I was frustrated by two points. First, while I'm no stranger to the kind of argumentation presented in the *Ethics*, there is a huge amount of historical context and secondary scholarship that I'm missing. Second, the printing I had acquired was riddled with typographical errors, even on the first page!

I decided to do something similar to what I did with [Ma'avar Yabok](../translation-of-maavar-yabok/), and use an LLM to create a freshly-translated, fully-annotated edition appropriate to my background. The process took a few days, and I am sharing the product here in the event that it is useful for any other interested reader. This time, I can offer a few distinct formats:
- PDF with translation on facing pages, suitable for screens  
[[`ethica.pdf`](../../s/ethica.pdf)]
- PDF with translation on facing pages, suitable for print (6" x 9")  
[[`ethica-print.pdf`](../../s/ethica-print.pdf)]
- Bilingual EPUB with interlinear translation, suitable for phones and tablets  
[[`ethica.epub`](../../s/ethica.epub)]
- Monolingual EPUBs, easier to read than the interlinear form  
[[`ethica-english.epub`](../../s/ethica-english.epub), [`ethica-latin.epub`](../../s/ethica-latin.epub)]

You can also read, browse, and search the entire book as a webpage here: [`benvlehmann.com/ethica/`](../../../ethica/).

This is not intended to be an academic critical edition, nor is it just a translation. On the one hand, textual accuracy is key: several distinct textual witnesses were used to compose this edition, and decisions made in the rendering of the Latin text are marked with extensive footnotes. On the other hand, it is designed to provide useful, nontrivial annotation and guidance to readers who are new to Spinoza. The book contains substantial expository content, including introductions to each of the five Parts; seven explanatory pieces interspersed at relevant points in the text; eleven appendices clarifying certain concepts; and reading aids such as summary tables and short restatements of the propositions. This edition also includes twenty-seven of Spinoza's letters that bear on the interpretation of the *Ethics*.

I am neither a Latinist nor a Spinozist, and cannot guarantee the accuracy of this edition. It is probably not appropriate for academic work. But I hope that it is useful for readers who are trying to understand the *Ethics* beyond a scholarly context.
